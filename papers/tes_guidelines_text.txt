SUBJECT : 2026 REVISED GUIDELINES ON THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THE TERTIARY EDUCATION SUBSIDY (TES)

RATIONALE

The UniFAST Board, by virtue of its authority to administer the Tertiary Education Subsidy (TES) program under Section 7 of Republic Act (RA) No. 10931, or the Universal Access to Quality Tertiary Education (UAQTE) Act, hereby issues these Revised Guidelines, hereinafter referred to as "Guidelines", to:

1. Reflect the necessary changes to TES policies approved by the Board; and,
2. Facilitate and expedite the TES implementation process effective 1st Semester of Academic Year (AY) 2026-2027 and onwards.

SECTION 1. COVERAGE

1. These Guidelines cover qualified students in Higher Education. TES for Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) shall be governed by separate guidelines issued by the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA). TESDA shall coordinate with the UniFAST Secretariat to ensure harmonized standards on eligibility verification, beneficiary prioritization, fund disbursement, monitoring, and reporting, consistent with the provisions of RA No. 10931 and its Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). TESDA-funded TVET beneficiaries shall also be included in the centralized cross-matching and validation systems to prevent duplication with other national government-funded Student Financial Assistance Programs (StuFAPs).

2. These Guidelines shall cover all State Universities and Colleges (SUCs), CHED-Recognized Local Universities and Colleges (LUCs), and Private Higher Education Institutions (PHEIs) that have Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) with CHED-UniFAST duly executed by the head of institution/campus, provided, that all program offerings are covered by Certificates of Program Compliance (COPCs), or at least a Report on the Result of Preliminary Assessment (RRPA) for new program offerings for SUCs and CHED-Recognized LUCs with Institutional Recognition (IR), and Government Recognition (GR) for PHEIs.

SECTION 2. DEFINITION OF TERMS

Terms appearing herein shall be understood as to how they are defined in RA No. 10931 and its IRR. In addition thereto, as used in these Guidelines:

1. Academic term refers to an entire length of time during which classes for a certain set of subjects are conducted, which could either be a semester or trimester as may be determined by the Higher Education Institution (HEI);
2. Academic year refers to either two (2) semesters including a midyear term or three (3) trimesters;
3. Comparable undergraduate degree refers to any higher education degree equivalent to Level 6, regardless of its recommended period of completion, based on the accepted outcomes-based learning and the Philippine Qualifications Framework (PQF);
4. Leave of absence (LOA) refers to the official withdrawal with intent to return by a student of his or her enrollment prior to or during the academic term;
5. Residence refers to a student's actual stay in a city or municipality for education purposes, whether in a rented unit or a dormitory, or with relatives;
6. TES-3A refers to the additional allowance for a student with a disability for expenses related to the student's disability, including special services, personal assistance, transportation, equipment, and supplies that are reasonably incurred;
7. TES-3B refers to the additional allowance for a student in a program requiring professional license or certification intended to cover the one (1)-time cost of obtaining the first professional credentials or qualifications, which may include the following: application fees, notarial fees, review classes fees, insurance premium fees, and documentation fees; and
8. Undergraduate post-secondary program refers to a bachelor's degree or comparable undergraduate degree taken after high school.

SECTION 3. STUDENT PRIORITIZATION

Prioritization for the allocation of new TES slots shall be in the following order, subject to availability of funds:

1. All enrolled students in SUCs, LUCs, and PHEIs, including Private HEIs in cities or municipalities with no SUCs or LUCs (PNSLs), who are part of households included in the most updated Listahanan. In case of discontinuance of Listahanan as certified by the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps) list, certified and provided by the DSWD, including dependents of solo parents, those who are part of Indigenous Peoples communities, and persons with disabilities, shall be applied in this prioritization, ranked according to household per capita income as determined by the DSWD; and

2. Pursuant to the Special Provision of the 2026 General Appropriations Act (GAA), all other enrolled students in SUCs, LUCs, and PHEIs, including PNSLs, who are not part of the prioritization category as mentioned in item no. 1, ranked according to household per capita income to be determined by the concerned agencies, subject to availability of funds, as follows:

2.1 Dependents of solo parents; and

Children or dependents of solo parents, as defined under Section 3(b) of RA No. 11861, or the Expanded Solo Parents Welfare Act, refer to those living with and dependent upon the solo parent for support who are unmarried, unemployed and 22 years old or below, or those over 22 years old but who are unable to fully take care or protect themselves from abuse, neglect, cruelty, exploitation, or discrimination because of a physical or mental disability or condition.

Under these Guidelines, applicants who are dependents of solo parents must be in

active status as dependents of solo parents during application to qualify as TES grantee. In addition, their identity must be duly reflected in the official DSWD Registry.

2.2 Those who are part of Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs).

Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) refer to a group of people or homogenous societies defined under Section 3(h) of RA No. 8371, or The Indigenous Peoples Rights Act (IPRA) of 1997. Basic Services (Section 25) of the IPRA provides that the State shall guarantee the right of ICCs/IPs to government's basic services which shall include, but not limited to, water and electrical facilities, education, health, and infrastructure.

Under these Guidelines, applicants must be in the list of households officially recognized as ICCs/IPs by the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP).

SECTION 4. AMOUNT OF SUBSIDY

Without prejudice to the power of the UniFAST Board to administer TES, including the determination of the amount of subsidy based on the annual budgetary appropriation for this purpose, the amount of subsidy shall be as follows:

1. TES grantees enrolled in SUCs and LUCs included in the prioritization category of Section 3(1) shall receive Ten Thousand Pesos (PhP10,000.00) per semester or Twenty Thousand Pesos (PhP20,000.00) per academic year.

2. TES grantees enrolled in private HEIs included in the prioritization category of Section 3(1) shall receive Thirteen Thousand Five Hundred Pesos (PhP13,500.00) per semester or Twenty-Seven Thousand Pesos (PhP27,000.00) per academic year.

3. TES grantees who are Persons with Disabilities (PWDs) under TES-3A shall receive an additional subsidy of Five Thousand Pesos (PhP5,000.00) per semester or Ten Thousand Pesos (PhP10,000.00) per academic year.

4. TES graduates in courses requiring licensure examinations shall receive TES-3B or a maximum one (1)-time reimbursement of Eight Thousand Pesos (PhP8,000.00) to cover the full or partial cost of taking the said licensure examinations.

5. TES grantees who belong under Section 3(2.1) and 3(2.2) of these Guidelines shall be given an allowance or subsidy of Five Thousand Pesos (PhP5,000.00) per semester or Ten Thousand Pesos (PhP10,000.00) per academic year. They shall be ranked according to household per capita income, as certified by the concerned agencies.

The amount of benefits provided to qualified grantees shall not be reduced and shall be continuously given for the duration of their undergraduate programs, and may be increased, subject to availability of funds, provided that the grantees remain compliant with the eligibility and retention requirements set by the participating HEIs.

SECTION 5. STUDENTS-AT-RISK OF DROPPING OUT (SARDO)

Pursuant to the Special Provision of the 2026 GAA, the UAQTE shall also cover the education benefits for students-at-risk of dropping out, subject to the Guidelines set by the UniFAST Board.

Students-at-risk of dropping out (SARDO) are students identified in the implementation of the Dropout Reduction Program (DORP) of the Department of Education (DepEd) pursuant to DepEd Order No. 74, series of 2010.

SARDO experience academic difficulties (Alzona & Villapando, 2018) and financial challenges (Jara, 2019) that increase their likelihood of dropping out. Timbal (2019) cited that predictive models can be used to identify these at-risk learners early, enabling the implementation of targeted interventions.

TES grantees who qualify to the SARDO allowance shall receive a one (1)-time additional subsidy of Ten Thousand Pesos (PhP10,000.00), subject to HEI evaluation.

The HEI, in the assessment of the one (1)-time SARDO applications of TES grantees, may be guided by these financial risk indicators:

Unexpected disruptions, such as death or serious illness of a primary income earner, households affected by natural calamities, pandemic, or other sudden economic crises, which adversely impact the grantees' ability to continue their studies.

The UniFAST Board shall issue separate guidelines for the implementation of TES for SARDO.

SECTION 6. TES FOR PNSL

The UniFAST Board shall set aside an amount to be allocated for TES PNSL. TES PNSL grantees shall receive a TES amount to be determined by the Board subject to the following:

A. A student enrolled in a private HEI within a city or municipality with no SUC/LUC, and the nearest of which is located at least 20 kilometers away from the said private HEI, shall be considered a TES-PNSL grantee.

B. A student enrolled in a private HEI within a city or municipality with no SUC/LUC, and the nearest of which is located within 20 kilometers from the said private HEI, shall likewise be considered a TES-PNSL grantee, provided that any of the following conditions apply:

1. The course or program of choice by the applicant is not offered in the SUC/LUC;
2. The course or program of choice by the applicant is offered in the SUC/LUC, but the applicant was not admitted due to admission or eligibility requirements; or
3. The course or program of choice by the applicant is offered in the SUC/LUC, but the student was not admitted due to absorptive capacity or slot limitations of the SUC/LUC.

Applicants must submit the official SUC/LUC notification on the result of their application. In cases where no notification was issued by the SUC/LUC, the concerned SUC/LUC must issue a Certification attesting to the applicable conditions, which shall form part of the documentary requirements for TES-PNSL eligibility.

The UniFAST Board shall issue separate guidelines for the implementation of TES for PNSL.

SECTION 8. DURATION OF ELIGIBILITY

The eligibility period for TES grantees begins in the academic year the TES award number is issued regardless of year level. This period is equivalent to the standard duration of the degree program, as prescribed in the curriculum, with an additional one (1)-year grace period. The academic years during which the student has availed of the TES shall be deducted from the remaining eligibility duration.

1. Grantee files for a LOA. The period of the LOA, during which no grant is disbursed, is excluded from the computation of the grantee's eligibility period. Consequently, the grantee's eligibility to the program is temporarily suspended during the LOA.

2. Transferee Grantee. For grantees who shift or transfer to a different program, the duration of eligibility will remain based on the original program in which they were initially enrolled, plus one (1) academic year as a grace period:

* Transfer to a program of equal duration: The eligibility period will remain aligned with the original program's required number of years for completion, plus a one (1)-year grace period.
* Transfer to a program of longer duration: The eligibility period will still be determined by the original program's required years for completion, plus a one (1)-year grace period, regardless of the extended duration of the new program.
* Transfer to a program with shorter duration: The eligibility period will be adjusted to the new program's required years for completion, plus a one (1)-year grace period.

1. Issuance of Call for Applications and Uploading of Applications by the HEIs. The

The schedule of submission of TES applications will be at the same time with the enrollment period of the HEI, as evidenced by the submitted academic calendar. 3. Name-Matching. The UniFAST Secretariat will match student-applicants based on the TES prioritization criteria. A list of qualified applicants is then finalized, and the results are released to the HEI through the HEI and Regional Coordinator (RC) Portals.

Applications for TES shall in no way be construed as automatic eligibility and qualification, as the same is subject to validation and availability of funds.

SECTION 10. VALIDATION AND VERIFICATION OF QUALIFIED APPLICANTS AND CONTINUING TES GRANTEES

3. Preparation of Certification, Master List, and Release of Funds. CHEDRO issues to the UniFAST Secretariat the Certification and Master List of TES Grantees, and other necessary documents for fund transfer for payment of TES.

B. VALIDATION OF CONTINUING TES GRANTEES. Continuing TES grantees, including returning grantees and transferees, shall be validated and verified by the UniFAST Regional Coordinators to be continuously eligible to TES based on existing processes in Sections 9 and 10(A), and documentary requirements stated in Section 11(C) of these Guidelines.

II. DISBURSEMENT

A. Disbursement of grants to qualified and validated grantees shall be by direct payment from CHEDROs to grantees in the following order of priority:

1. Direct credit to the account of the grantees through LDDAP-ADA;
2. Check payments; and
3. In the event that (1) and (2) are not applicable, payments to grantees could be coursed through the HEIs.

All CHEDROs/HEIs must disburse the received benefits to students within **15 working days** upon receipt of the funds. CHEDROs/HEIs must submit a Certification on the completed disbursement of funds to the grantees within **seven (7) working days** after completion of disbursement (Annex 5).

B. In cases where the payment will be coursed through the HEIs:

(a) Any TES grant not received by the grantee within **90 calendar days** upon receipt of the HEIs, such as in cases where the TES grantee cannot be located, shall be returned to the CHEDRO for liquidation.
(b) Failure to disburse said funds will result in the non-release of succeeding Management Fee until such time that the funds are fully disbursed. The CHEDRO shall notify the concerned HEI on the withholding of the Management Fee. Repeated delay or non-release of TES to grantees shall be a ground for termination of the MOA with the HEI as stated in Section 15 of these Guidelines.
(c) HEIs must maintain a separate bank account with the Land Bank of the Philippines (LBP) for proper account management of TES.

SECTION 13. MANAGEMENT FEE

A Management Fee of 1% will be applied to the total TES amount for students enrolled in participating HEIs, excluding TES-3A, TES-3B, and TES for SARDO.

The Management Fee shall be divided equally between the HEI where the student is enrolled and the CHEDRO with jurisdiction over the HEI.

The Management Fee is allocated to cover administrative expenses related to the implementation of TES program. To ensure accurate disbursement and liquidation, HEIs must comply with the following requirements:

* **For Disbursement:** Submit the original copy of the OR upon receiving the TES amount.
* **For Liquidation:** Provide a PDF copy of the OR.

SECTION 15. SANCTIONS AND PENALTIES

After proper procedures were undertaken to conduct a fact-finding investigation of reported violations committed by any partner public or private HEI implementing the TES, particularly concerning compliance with any provisions of these Guidelines, or related government accounting and audit regulations, the UniFAST Board, on the recommendation of the Executive Director, may impose any of the following sanctions, depending on the gravity of the violation:

1. Non-inclusion in the list of qualified HEIs to implement the TES, without prejudice to any measures that may be undertaken by the UniFAST Board to maintain the TES availment of said HEI's qualified continuing TES grantees;

2. Upon deliberation of the UniFAST Board, termination of the MOA for fraud, repeated non-liquidation of funds (failure to liquidate funds on at least two [2] semesters), and unjust non-release of TES;

3. Recommendation to the CHED *En Banc* to impose particular sanction to the HEI for committing violations under RA No. 10931, after due process pursuant to RA No. 7722;

4. For serious violations that would merit the exercise of its authority, filing of criminal, civil, or administrative case against the erring official of the HEI, when warranted; and

5. Such other penalties or sanctions that may be approved by the UniFAST Board.

SECTION 16. PARTICIPATION TO MONITORING AND EVALUATION ACTIVITIES, ADVOCACY PROMOTIONS, AND TRANSPARENCY INITIATIVES

The Secretariat and CHEDROs shall regularly conduct monitoring and evaluation activities, and advocacy promotions campaigns to ensure that information about the TES, including but not limited to its benefits, terms of availment, and processing period, shall attain the widest reach and are known to participating HEIs and TES grantees. All participating HEIs shall participate in all monitoring and evaluation activities, advocacy promotions, and transparency initiatives (on-site liquidation, ceremonial and actual payout of TES grants to students).

SECTION 17. INTERPRETATION CLAUSE

All doubts in the implementation, interpretation, and construction of the provisions of these Guidelines and the provisions of RA No. 10931 and its IRR relating to TES shall be resolved in favor of a deserving TES grantee and the optimized utilization of government resources.

SECTION 18. SEPARABILITY CLAUSE

Should any part of these Guidelines be declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other parts or provisions hereof not affected shall continue to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 19. REPEALING CLAUSE

All other existing issuances, memorandum circulars, orders, and memoranda that are contrary to or inconsistent with any of the provisions of this Memorandum Circular shall be deemed repealed or modified accordingly. All other provisions not affected by this amendment shall remain in full force and effect.

SECTION 20. EFFECTIVITY CLAUSE

These Guidelines shall take effect starting the 1st Semester of AY 2026-2027.
